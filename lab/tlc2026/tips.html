Soldering tips

Lead-free solder

Our kits use lead-free solder. It works a little differently from leaded solder.

  • Hotter: lead-free melts at about 217°C, leaded at about 183°C. Set the iron to roughly 350°C (660°F) for lead-free, versus roughly 315°C (600°F) for leaded.
  • Duller: good lead-free joints look matte or slightly grainy. Leaded joints look shiny. Don't judge a joint by shine alone; judge it by shape.
  • Wash your hands after soldering, and don't eat or drink at the bench.

Through-hole joints

Through-hole parts have legs that go through the board. Solder them on the back.

  1. Push the part in flat against the board and bend the legs slightly so it stays put.
  2. Touch the iron to the pad and the leg together for 1–2 seconds.
  3. Feed solder onto the pad and leg (not onto the iron) until it flows around both.
  4. Pull the solder away, then the iron. Don't move the part until the solder sets.
  5. Trim the leg just above the joint.

The shape we want: a small smooth cone, like a tiny volcano. The solder should cover the whole pad, curve inward toward the leg, and taper up to it. Not a round ball sitting on top, and not a flat splat.

Surface-mount parts

Surface-mount parts are tiny and sit flat on the pads, with no legs through the board. Our SAO has a few, such as the LEDs and resistors.

  1. Check the part's orientation first. LEDs have a polarity, so make sure it matches the board.
  2. Put a small bit of solder on one pad only.
  3. Hold the part with tweezers, reheat that pad, and slide the part into place. Let go once the solder sets.
  4. Solder the other pad: touch the iron to the pad and the part, add a little solder.
  5. Reheat the first pad if it needs tidying, then check both sides.

Use as little solder as you can. Too much makes a blob that can bridge across pads.

Cold joints

A cold joint happens when the solder never melted properly or the metal wasn't hot enough. The solder sits on the surface instead of bonding to the pad and leg.

  • Looks like: a ball or blob, a lumpy or cracked surface, or a gap around the leg.
  • Why it matters: it might work, then stop when you bump the badge. A flaky connection is the most common reason a kit doesn't light up.
  • Fix: reheat the joint until the solder flows, and add a touch of fresh solder if needed.

Removing solder

Made a mistake, bridged two pads, or put a part in the wrong way? Solder wick (desoldering braid) soaks up the extra solder. We'll have some at the table, so just ask.

  1. Lay the braid flat on the joint.
  2. Press the hot iron on top of the braid for a second or two.
  3. The solder melts and gets pulled up into the braid.
  4. Lift the braid and iron away together, then cut off the used section.
